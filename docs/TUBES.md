# The Treeline

Two public plots, **C1** and **N1**, hold the stations of the Treeline, one glass tube for people and parcels: Hedgerow Halt on C1 and Willow Halt on N1. Each station takes a single plot, so it removes no road and no lamp. Both plots are excluded from the builder and contribution validation, which leaves 141 house plots.

A 5-pixel glass tube lies low behind the northwest tree line, 8 pixels off the ground, and shows only in the gaps between the trees. It fades as the map zooms out and never carries snow. Each station is one slim glass stack, 12 by 48 pixels, with a stone pad at its foot and a small lamp in its hood, standing at the front of a plot that stays a meadow. From each stack a Γ-shaped spur runs out at 39 pixels over the lawn and the west lane, then dips to the trunk behind the trees. Hedgerow Halt also has a tiny pale-green enamel sign and an umbrella stand. Open `#venue=tube`, or click a stack, the sign or the glass. The camera frames the station with its spur and the trunk behind it, beside the desktop panel or above the mobile panel, and the panel says who is on the line now, how many rides the day holds, when the next one leaves, and where the parcel is.

## Riding only when it saves time

A neighbor rides only when, door to door, the tube is at least ten unhurried minutes faster than walking the whole way (`TUBE_MIN_SAVING`). The tube's minutes are fixed: 2 to board, which includes the 0.7-tile walk from the road to the stack, the turn, the crouch and the fwoomp; 5.45 in the glass, 54.5 tiles at 10 tiles a minute; and 2 to step off, settle and walk back out to the road. That is 9.45 minutes from Hedgerow Halt's door to Willow Halt's, against 150 on foot.

The choice is unhurried and ignores the timetable, so it is the same for a home and a destination every day, in both directions. Over every house plot and every destination (the stage, the green, the cinema, the football, the Millpond and the zoo), exactly 151 pairs ride. A trip's saving moves in steps of 12.5 minutes less the fixed 9.45, so there is a wide gap: the best trip that stays on foot would save only 3.05 minutes, and the smallest ride saves 15.55. Nearby trips stay on foot.

Only walking hurries, never past 1.4 times the usual pace; the glass keeps its own speed. Because ten minutes is more than 0.4 × 9.45, the tube stays faster even at full hurry, so a trip that rides never fails where walking the whole way would have made it. The way home is the same choice, never decided again. Every trip before a neighbor's first ride of the day is planned exactly as it would be on foot with the same outings, and a test compares the two plans. A rider's last walk joins the venue's own path where the road first meets it, so nobody walks past the zoo's gate and back; from Willow Halt that saves 22.2 tiles.

Over a calendar year of today's 18 homes, 435 of the 3,137 planned trips ride, by 11 of the 18 neighbors, and each rides both ways: 870 rides. Most go to the zoo (275), then to the stage's concerts (74), the green (48) and the cinema (38). Seats go down each day's line to the next neighbor who can make it, and a far neighbor whose turn comes keeps it by riding, where without the tube the seat would pass to the next neighbor who can walk. So the tube changes who goes as well as how: without it the town would plan 2,983 trips, and on 101 of the 112 days someone who doesn't ride that day would spend it differently. Of the trips the guests make, 238 fit only by tube, and none is lost: a neighbor gets a seat only when every outing they already have still fits. The zoo has 336 visits instead of 324, each about 55 minutes longer on average, so the time spent at the zoo grows from about 30,000 to 50,000 minutes a year. A day has 4 to 10 rides, 7.8 on average, from the first boarding at 12:01 to the last stepping off at 23:49, and no ride on today's roster crosses midnight. At most three neighbors are on the line at once, and at most two stand in one stack.

With two stations the line is one straight run, and the rule takes it at its word: a neighbor may walk the wrong way to a station, or step off past the destination and walk back, whenever the whole trip still saves ten minutes. Today's roster does both. The zoo-goers on D2, D4, E2 and F2 walk 4 to 12 tiles north to Hedgerow Halt to ride south, about 100 trips a year, and still save 28 to 78 minutes each way. The neighbor on Q3 rides north to Hedgerow Halt for the cinema and walks back south to its door, 38 trips a year, still saving 28 minutes. Empty plots add more: football fans from O1–T1 would ride north past the Meadow Ground and walk back south to its gate, saving 15.55 minutes, and neighbors on rows J–M would walk south to Willow Halt to ride north to the stage and the green. A station at G1 would end the O1–T1 football walk-back, but a trial build shows it brings its own: O1–T1 would ride to G1 for the Millpond and walk 8 tiles back south, and football fans on L1 and M1–M5 would walk south to Willow Halt to ride north to G1. See [Growing the line](#growing-the-line).

## The stations and the sign

Hedgerow Halt on C1 is the first station and carries the sign; Willow Halt on N1 is the second. Each stack stands 0.7 tiles in from the plot's doorstep. Hedgerow Halt's door is on road y = 13, which runs east to the Lunch Green, and Willow Halt's on road y = 57, which runs straight east to the zoo's north gate.

The sign reads "People & parcels. Please remove umbrella." word for word. It is painted in three lines, "People & parcels.", "Please remove" and "umbrella.", in bold 4-pixel Space Mono on a 44 × 15 plate of pale lawn-green enamel that faces the road, just right of the stack. At the town's usual zoom it is a small green plate; the lettering reads from about zoom 3. The panel is where it is read: it shows the same words as real text on a plate in the sign's own colors, which it keeps at night because it is a physical sign. Willow Halt has no sign.

West of Hedgerow Halt's walk-in stands a small brass bucket with one furled plum umbrella, crook handle up, so every neighbor boarding there passes it.

## Boarding, riding and stepping off

Boarding takes two minutes. The neighbor walks from the door to the stack, back to the camera, at 0.4375 tiles a minute, and steps in at 1.6 minutes. Inside the glass they turn to face out, crouch at 1.8, and at 1.88 the fwoomp stretches them up the glass and into the hood by 2.0. Two rings of air and four bits of grass puff out around the stack's foot for half a minute.

In the glass they are a tiny figure in their own colors, head first, with a faint streak of those colors behind. They whoosh along the spur, dip behind the trees at the elbow and run down the trunk at 10 tiles a minute, a 5.45-minute ride from one stack to the other. The glass washes over them, so they read as inside it. The little figure is straight, so where the glass turns (a stack's top, the corner over the lawn, the dip and the elbow) it is cut short at the turn rather than sticking out into the air.

At the far stack they drop down the glass in 0.12 minutes, with another puff, settle by 0.25, and walk out to the door by 2.0, toward the camera, at 0.4 tiles a minute.

Two at once: riders within 0.45 tiles of each other in the glass are drawn one just behind the other, two figures in a stack stand side by side 3 pixels apart and each crouches a moment after the one before, and two walking in or out together keep 3 pixels apart. Riders going opposite ways would pass through each other in the single bore. On today's roster their times in the glass never overlap, so it happens 0 times a year; a bigger roster can bring a crossing, which lasts about a frame and is accepted. With every house plot taken (the made-up town `npm run check:full-town` builds), 45 neighbors ride 2,654 times a year, riders cross 84 times, up to six are in the glass at once, and up to four stand side by side in one stack.

The labels read "Boarding the tube to Willow Grove Zoo", "Riding the tube to Willow Grove Zoo", "Boarding the tube home from the zoo", "Riding the tube home from the zoo" and "Stepping off the tube at Willow Halt", with the cinema, the football, the Millpond and other events named the same way. They show in the follow status, the neighbors list and the house card. Walking legs keep their usual labels.

## Parcels

Parcels ride too. A daylight timetable offers a slot every 20 minutes from 07:00 to 19:00, each leaving up to six minutes late by a seeded hash of the day, alternately from the north and the south; about one slot in four stays empty. A parcel waits on its pad for half a minute, rises into the stack, rides stack to stack in 5.45 minutes, drops onto the far pad and waits there half a minute to be collected. A slot is skipped whenever its episode would come within a minute of anyone boarding, riding or stepping off, so a parcel never shares the glass, a stack or a pad with a person. The last one is collected by 19:12, before the night begins at 20:00, so no parcels run at night. That leaves 20 to 28 parcels a day, 24.5 on average, about 2,750 a year. Parcels get no puff. The panel says when the next one leaves, or that one is waiting, in the glass or just arrived.

## Quiet on purpose

- **Behind the trees.** The trunk runs at x = −0.5, just behind the northwest tree line, with its centerline 8 pixels up (underside 5.5, top 10.5). It is painted with the ground, before the town's depth sort, so every tree and everything else in town stands in front of it, and it shows only in the gaps. Riders on the trunk and the two elbows are painted straight after the ground, before the sort. Ten trunk posts, 6 pixels tall, stand every four tiles, and one 27-pixel post holds each spur in the tree-free tile beside the lane.
- **It fades with distance.** The glass is drawn at `clamp((zoom − 0.3) / 1, 0.35, 1)`: 0.35 at the whole-town zoom, 0.40 at the opening view's 0.7, and full from 1.3. The stacks, the sign, the stand, the posts, the pads and the riders themselves stay at full strength; the whoosh is how the line is found. The discoverability floor is tested: at the opening zoom the glass highlight still stands at least 40 levels off the lawn (43.3 today).
- **Hover and selection.** Hovering either station draws the glass at full strength, recolors its 1-pixel highlight along the whole line to pale gold at no extra draw calls, and washes both station plots. Selecting either station adds a full pale-gold halo along the whole route, trunk included.
- **Low and small.** A stack with its hood is 48 pixels tall, 49 with snow, and the tests hold it to 50. The sign is 25 pixels, the umbrella 17, and the spur runs 36.5–41.5 pixels above the lawn, so a 31-pixel walker on the lane clears its underside by 5.5.
- **The meadow stays.** The station plots keep their wildflowers, and the daisies still grow under the spur. Only the sprout stake, the plot label and the dashed outline are left out.
- **Amber stays with light.** The glass stays cool, day and night. The only amber is the lit lamp in each hood and its glow. See [the amber budget](BRAND.md#the-amber-budget).
- **No crowd promised.** The panel names neighbors only while they are on the line, and never names anyone before they ride. Two neighbors who share a name read "Two neighbors named Jon are on the line.", never "Jon and Jon". Headings end with a period, eyebrows are uppercase, and there are no exclamation marks.

## Night and winter

The lamp in each hood lights in the streetlamp wave, between 20:20 and 20:30, by its distance from the Fork, with two modest glows, one at the hood and one at the pad. The glass takes its night colors, with the highlight at about 45%, and stays cool. The sign keeps its colors and its ink is never brightened. A late rider standing in the lit stack is the nicest frame of the night.

In winter, snow lies on three surfaces only: the hood, the top of the sign and the bucket's rim, faded in and out with the town's snow cover. There is no snow on the glass or the posts, and the meadow's frost shows through the glass. Autumn's golden hour washes over the line as it does over everything else.

## Following a rider

- **On the map.** Follow a neighbor from the neighbors list, and while they board, ride and step off the camera follows the figure itself: up the stack, along the glass and down again. The height is continuous across every stage, so the map never jumps. The follow status reads their label.
- **On `/live`.** Riders can be followed like anyone outdoors. While a neighbor rides, the camera eases with a 0.12-second time constant instead of 1.6, so the glass stays in frame at 10 tiles a second, and it settles as before once they step off. The shot center and the name label rise with them, so neither dips. See [the live broadcast](LIVE.md).
- **In the glass.** There is no foot diamond in the glass; a followed rider's glass glows faintly gold instead. In a stack the diamond sits at the stack's foot.
- **Clicks.** Riders in the glass and figures in a stack cannot be clicked; a click there selects the station. Neighbors walking to and from a stack can be clicked as usual.

## Growing the line

`TUBE_STATIONS` stays in row order, north to south, so a new station is inserted where it falls along the line rather than appended (a test holds it). The model already handles any number of stations: `tubeChoice` tries every pair, and `tubeRoute` builds any pair from its two spurs and the trunk between them. So does the art. What a new station still needs:

- **The art's geometry.** Nothing to do. `src/city/tubes.ts` cuts its glass from every station's own spur and the runs of trunk between them, and hands each ride to its pieces by station and direction, so a new station's spur is drawn, sorted and hit, and a ride between any two stations stays in its own glass. It throws, rather than drawing a rider in the wrong glass, if a station ever lacks its spur.
- **The trunk posts.** They stand on every block's middle row along the trunk, four tiles apart, and skip each station's own row, so a station in the middle loses the post on its row (y = 27.5 for G1) and gets a glass bubble there over its T instead. Both happen by themselves.
- **The sign.** The sign, the panel's sign caption and `TUBE_VENUE` follow `TUBE_STATIONS[0]`, the northernmost station, which today is Hedgerow Halt. No station planned on the trunk lies north of C1, but the east branch would sort before it, so pin the sign to Hedgerow Halt when that branch is built.
- **The pinned numbers.** The route's 40 points and 54.498 tiles, the 151 riding pairs and the gap, and the 141 house plots are pinned in `tests/tubes.test.ts` and in the counts listed under [Code and verification](#code-and-verification), and each moves with a new station.

The next station is planned but not built:

- **G1, Meadow Halt.** Its stack would stand at (3.5, 28.8) and its spur run along row 27.5. The column-0 pieces there sort at depth 26.9, behind the row-27 edge tree. Leave out the column-0 spur post on any row with an edge tree, or move it to x ≈ 0.9, and check 26.9 against the tree's 1.48 scale and ±7-pixel jitter; failing that, skip that one edge tree. The T-bubble at (−0.5, 27.5) replaces the trunk post there by itself. `HOUSE_PLOTS` drops from 141 to 140. Re-run the pairs and gap test, and test the walks [above](#riding-only-when-it-saves-time): G1 ends the O1–T1 football walk-back but adds the Millpond one from O1–T1 and the walk south for football fans on L1 and M1–M5.
- **Rows to the south.** The trunk continues, and Willow Halt's elbow becomes a T.
- **Columns to the east.** A branch along the other back edge, y = −0.5, behind the northeast trees, with a corner elbow at (0, 0), stations on A5 or A6 mirrored, and the sign's skew flipped. The line is then no longer one run, so the order rule and the sign's station are revisited with it.

## Determinism

Everything on the line is a pure function of the places, the town day and the minute, like the rest of the simulation. One route geometry, `tubeRoute`, a 3-D polyline of ground tiles and centerline heights, is read by both the renderer and the model, so a rider is always drawn inside the glass. The route, the tube choice for each start and end, and each day's rides per roster are memoized, but all are pure, so no answer depends on what was asked before. The parcel timetable seeds from `hash()` of fixed `tube-parcel:` strings. There is no `Math.random`, no `Date`, no storage and no canvas created at module load, so node tests can import every module. Everyone sees the same rides at the same moment, even after a refresh.

## Performance

Measured with the recording context in node, where the ground layer is counted because there is no cache. `drawGlow` does nothing in node, so a lit lamp adds two `drawImage` calls in the browser that these numbers leave out.

- In the opening view at 1440 × 900 (camera 720, 88, zoom 0.7), with Hedgerow Halt in view, the line draws 163 calls a frame against a budget of 250: 45 in the ground layer and 118 in depth objects. The spur glass takes 84, the stack 10, the sign 10, the umbrella stand 6, the corner bubble 5 and the spur post 3. Winter adds one call each for the hood, the sign and the bucket.
- The ground cache holds the pads, the trunk posts, and the glass of the elbows and the trunk with its halo and hover highlight. It reads only `night`, the emphasis, the zoom and the view, never the minute, so the riders, the parcels, the lamp and the snow are drawn every frame.
