# Football at the Meadow Ground

The football ground was added at the 100-plot expansion; the town now has 200 plots. Six new, unoccupied plots — F3, F4, F5, G3, G4, G5 — form one public ground beside the original neighborhood. The pitch has striped grass, white markings, two netted goals, corner flags, sponsor boards, two dugouts with a manager each, floodlight masts, a refreshment cart, a crowd rail, and an elevated scoreboard. Its internal roads, streetlights, trees, and plot outlines are removed; roads around its perimeter remain connected. All six plots are excluded from the builder and shared contribution validation.

## A match worth stopping for

Meadow FC wear green; Sunset United wear amber. It is five-a-side: each team has a goalkeeper in a contrasting shirt, a defender, two wings and a striker, all named, with numbers on their backs. Games repeat from 06:00 until 20:00 town time. Each cycle takes 140 real seconds: a 60-second first half, an eight-second half-time, a 60-second second half, then 12 seconds at full-time before the next kick-off. The scoreboard presents this as a ten-minute mini-match. Six matches fit into daylight exactly.

Every match is played out by a small football engine rather than scripted. Players keep a shape, support the ball carrier, press and mark, and choose between passing (short, through, switched, lofted, crossed, or back to the keeper), dribbling past an opponent, and shooting. Defenders tackle, poke the ball away and intercept; keepers claim crosses, dive, catch, parry, tip shots round the post or over the bar, and throw, roll or punt the ball out. A typical game has about a dozen shots: long efforts, headers from corners, blocks and deflections, and rebounds pounced on after a parry, with roughly one in five going in. Balls leave play for throw-ins, corners and goal kicks, hit the woodwork, bulge the net, and stop at the boards and the crowd rail. A goal is scored only when the ball crosses the goal line between the posts and under the bar, in play; the scorer runs to their own supporters and the other side kicks off. At half-time the teams swap ends in lanes that never meet and take a drink, while a player or the referee brings the ball back to the centre spot; at full-time they shake hands, wave, and line up exactly for the next kick-off. After the day's last game they head for their dugouts instead, and the referee keeps the ball. A ball already rolling when the whistle goes is stopped by whoever it reaches, the keeper first.

There are no fouls, offsides, injuries, extra time, persistent league, user control, or multiplayer. Everything that happens is deterministic: the UTC calendar date and match number seed an integer random generator, and the engine uses only exact arithmetic, so every browser reaches the same decisions to the last bit. Each match is simulated once, in about 40 ms, and recorded at 20 frames a second; the state at any moment interpolates the two frames around it. The same six-match lineup repeats in each 24-minute town day throughout that UTC date, with a new lineup at midnight UTC. The UTC clock chooses the match and its exact elapsed time, so viewers with agreeing device clocks see the same score, ball, players, commentary, and sound cues regardless of timezone, reloads, or arrival time. No per-visit randomness or accumulated state is involved. Both the interactive town and the live broadcast use the same function. Pause freezes the view; resuming rejoins the live match. Reduced-motion preferences start the town paused.

Eight public supporters make the touchline feel alive. Like the stage performers, these are town scenery, not contributed residents. Up to six eligible morning or afternoon strollers can also watch, selected from half of the available walkers. Scheduled lunch guests take priority; work, home, and sleep routines are respected. Visitors enter from the south perimeter road, stay outside the touchline, and return to their own doorsteps before their next routine period. Their directory status says they are watching football.

## Watch and listen

Choose **Town events → football**, click the ground, its scoreboard or any of its furniture, or open `#venue=football`. The camera fits the ground beside the desktop panel or above the mobile panel. The panel shows team colors, the score and scorers, the match clock and a timeline, running commentary, key moments, shots, shots on target, saves, corners and possession, the line-ups, the day's earlier results, and the crowd count. Zoom buttons keep the selected pitch centered as you get closer.

Use **Town sound → Turn sound on** to enable sound. Kicks, whistles, the final whistle, cheers, groans at near misses, the ring of the woodwork, and applause are short original synthesized effects sharing the town volume control. They fade with zoom and distance from the center of the view, pan toward the pitch, and fall silent when the ground is off screen. No audio starts without a click. Muting, pausing, or hiding the page stops effects; reconnecting or seeking skips missed sounds instead of replaying them in a burst. The floodlights come on for the last game of the day. The ground is quiet at night while the Little Stage keeps its own program.

## Implementation and checks

- `src/lib/football-engine.ts`: the match engine. `simulateMatch(day, match)` plays one match at 20 Hz and returns its recorded frames (players, referee, ball, possession, restarts), events with commentary, sound cues, statistic marks and goals.
- `src/lib/football.ts`: the shared contract (`FootballState` and its parts), the reserved site and geometry, the rosters, a small cache of recorded matches, `footballAt(minutes, day)` for any moment, the paced commentary line, and camera audibility.
- `src/city/football.ts`: pitch, goals, boards, dugouts, masts, scoreboard, players, referee, ball, crowd, and furnishings. Objects share the town's depth sorting; long objects (the boards and the scoreboard) are drawn in depth-keyed slices.
- `src/lib/resident-trips.ts`: contributor spectator trips on the existing road graph.
- `src/components/FootballMatch.tsx`: accessible live match detail; `TownEvents.tsx` provides keyboard entry.
- `src/music/football-sound.ts`, `football-worker.ts`, `player.ts`, and `Soundtrack.tsx`: effect synthesis (rendered in a worker when sound is turned on), playback lifecycle, and proximity gating.

Run `npm run check` and `npm run format:check`. Tests cover the reserved plots and roads, determinism across timezones, reloads and computation order, the exact line-ups at every kick-off, continuous movement at human speeds inside the ground, balls always called when they leave play, goals only between the posts and under the bar, no ball crossing a goal mouth or passing through a player without being played, saves only by a keeper who is really there, kick-offs that are not a set move, the balance of shots, saves and goals over many matches, nothing rolling in after a whistle, one wind-up per throw, statistics that agree with the events, varied commentary, spectator routines, sound envelopes and cue playback, proximity, and pause/mute cleanup.

With the development server running, `/tests/manual/football.html` provides a daylight match preview with pause, time, zoom, next match, night view, and explicit sound controls. It exercises the real renderer and simulation without changing the live town clock or house files.
