# The Millpond

Eight public plots, **H3, H4, H5, H6, I3, I4, I5, I6**, form one long pond south of the Meadow Ground. The internal streets are removed, while the perimeter roads remain connected and keep all their lamps. These eight plots are excluded from the builder and contribution validation, which leaves 141 house plots.

A stone-footed watermill stands on the south-west bank, its wheel turning in a small bay. A wooden jetty runs out from the south shore, reeds grow thick in the east cove, and a worn path follows the south bank from a gate on the south road. Further east along that road, near the south-east corner, a small timber plaque on two short posts reads "The Millpond". The mill's windows stay dark: nobody lives there. Its wheel turns once every six town minutes, six real seconds, whenever the water is open. Open `#venue=millpond`, or click the water, the mill or the gate sign. The camera fits the pond beside the desktop panel or above the mobile panel, and the panel says what the water is doing now: the ice and skating times, the rowboats, the heron, the fisher, rising fish, and whatever the season has left on the water.

## Through the year

The pond keeps the town's calendar (see [The turning year](THE_TURNING_YEAR.md)). The bank, the water, the ice, the lily pads and the jetty deck are ground art, which reads the season only as a whole day, so the year reaches them at midnight; everything that moves is drawn every frame.

- **Ice.** The pond freezes with the river, on the same days. Over Winter 4–8 a rim of ice grows in from the banks, a day at a time. Pans of ice also form in the coves and grow with it. From Winter 9 to Winter 19 it is frozen hard: a pale sheet with a faint sheen, faint arcs of skate tracks around each loop, and patches of snow once the town's snow has settled. Over Winter 20–23 it breaks into floes that shrink and drift apart, and Winter 24 is open water again. While there is any ice the wheel stands still, the fish, the fisher and the lantern glints stay away, and the heron does not hunt. The heron itself is away only while the pond is frozen hard.
- **Petals.** From Spring 15 to Spring 27, while the trees shed, up to fourteen blossom petals drift across the water.
- **Lily pads.** Each pad opens on its own day between Spring 21 and Spring 28 and goes between Autumn 11 and Autumn 21. About one pad in three flowers pink, opening between Summer 3 and Summer 8 and going by Summer 19–26.
- **Rowboats.** Two rowboats lie upturned side by side on the west bank beside the mill from Autumn 21 to Spring 9, keel up, with snow on their bottoms in the snowy weeks. They wait at the jetty from Spring 10. From Summer 1 to Autumn 6 they go out: the first pushes off at 08:30, backing out stern first before it turns, each takes ten minutes to glide out onto its loop and ten to come back, and both are tied up again by 18:30. The rowers are scenery, not neighbours, and a small wake follows each boat. A boat is put away or brought back at midnight, fading over 23:50–00:10.
- **Reed fireflies.** On summer nights five small swarms hover over the reed beds and the water beside them, on the town's own firefly schedule and in the same green-gold. They never use the glow sprite.
- **Floating leaves.** From Autumn 14 fallen leaves land on the water, each on its own day, and drift in over three days to gather along the banks. They are gone by Winter 2, before the first ice.
- **Reeds.** Thirteen reed beds, five of them in the east cove. In early spring new shoots come up through last year's straw, and the beds are green from Spring 13. Cattails stand in them from Summer 8. They turn to straw from Autumn 3 and pale further from Winter 1, and their seed heads take a cap of snow. The reeds have their own colours (`REED`), never the trees' foliage or the fallen-leaf swatches.

## Dawn and dusk

- **Mist.** Every morning, 05:30–07:00, thin mist gathers over the water in five low banks that drift slowly along together, thickest at 06:15. Over ice it is half as thick. Its colour is its own, distinct from snow and ice.
- **The early fisher.** On open-water mornings, 05:30–07:00, a fisher sits at the end of the jetty with their back to the town. The line goes out a few minutes after they arrive and comes in before they leave. The bobber dips once each morning, at its own minute between 05:45 and 06:40, and on one morning in three a small fish comes in. The fisher is scenery, never a neighbour, and carries no lantern.
- **The heron's hunts.** On open water the heron wades out at 05:30 and hunts the south shallows 06:00–08:00. It hunts again in the east cove 16:00–18:30, and at 21:00 it walks back to its roost.
- **Fish at dusk.** The fish rise most between 18:30 and 20:30, about twice a minute, and about a third of them leap.

## The heron and the fish

The ducks stay with the river. The pond's wildlife is a grey heron and the fish it hunts.

The heron lives on the pond all year except while the pond is frozen hard. It sleeps on one leg in the east-cove reeds, neck tucked in, from 21:30 to 05:30. Between its hunts it stands or preens at a few spots in the south-east shallows, walking slowly between them. Hunting, it stalks at under 0.05 tiles a town minute, freezes, and strikes. Each hunt has three to five strikes at seeded minutes, and about two in five end with a silver fish in the bill, swallowed head back. It turns its head toward a fish that leaps within two tiles. While it wades, its legs disappear at the waterline. It never flies, it moves continuously from minute to minute, and it keeps clear of the jetty, the boats, the wheel bay and the landing. On freezing and thawing days, Winter 4–8 and Winter 20–23, it does not hunt. It steps out of its roost at 05:30 and walks west across the cove, onto the edge of the ice on a freezing day, or into the first open water past the thin rim of ice on a thawing day. It stands and preens there all day, within a tile and a half of its roost, and walks back at 21:00. It is away only on the frozen days: asleep at its roost, it fades out over 23:50–00:10 into Winter 9, and fades back in the same way into Winter 20.

On open water the fish rise from 06:00. A rise is a flat ring that spreads and fades over about 1.2 town minutes. By day about one minute in three brings a rise. Over 18:00–18:30 the rate climbs to about two a minute, a second rise coming half a minute into each minute, and holds until 20:30. It eases off through 21:30, and a few quiet rings come in the moonlight until 23:00. At dusk two or three rings are usually spreading at once. Before 21:30, about one rise in three is a leap: a small fish, dark along the back, arcs out of its ring and drops back in 8 pixels to one side, where a second ring spreads with two drops of water. Rises stay half a tile from the shore and clear of the jetty, the boats, the heron, and anything the mill would hide, and so does where each leap lands.

## Skating

On frozen days, Winter 9–19, the pond is open for skating from 14:00 to 16:40.

- **Neighbours.** Afternoon strollers who are not already at the Lunch Green, the zoo or the football that afternoon may skate. Half of them, rounded up and at most six, are chosen for the day by a stable hash of the day and their house ID. They walk the roads to the south gate, follow the shore path until they are level with their own loop, and walk straight out onto the ice. Each has one of six loops, and the loops in full view fill first: the first skater takes the loop just west of the jetty, and the next four the loops across the middle and east of the pond. The sixth loop, tucked in beside the mill, is reached across the ice from the second skater's line, so nobody ever walks behind the mill. A skater starts gliding the moment they reach the south of their loop, a few minutes before the posted 14:00 (about 13:55 for the first, and up to six and a half minutes earlier for the others), glides whole laps and steps off at the same point between about 16:33 and 16:40. Everyone is off the ice by 16:40, and they walk home the way they came, in time for their next routine. Their status reads "Walking to the Millpond", "Skating on the Millpond" and "Walking home from the Millpond", and on the ice they lean forward with their arms out, on pale blades. With today's houses that is usually one neighbour on the ice each frozen afternoon; the number grows with the town.
- **Scenery skaters.** Two more figures skate small loops in the east cove over the same hours, fading in and out over five minutes. Like the stage performers, they are scenery rather than contributed residents, and the panel never counts them.
- **Where it shows.** The Events panel shows a **Skating on the Millpond** card on skating days, and while skating is live it adds how many neighbours are on the ice. The pond's panel says when skating starts and ends and, while it is live, who is out on the ice: one or two neighbours by name, or how many when there are more. Skating is not a scheduled venue event: it follows the ice, as Lantern hour follows nightfall. See [Town events](TOWN_EVENTS.md). On `/live` a neighbour out on the ice can always be followed, whatever the day's highlights, because the pond has no show of its own to skip. See [the live broadcast](LIVE.md).

## The moon and the lanterns

- **The moon in the water.** While the moon is up, 20:00–06:00, its reflection lies on the pond in the same phase and brightness as the almanac's moon, cut from the sky's own lunar slices. It is broken into rows of light 1 pixel high with dark water between them, with no disc behind, and a short broken path of six fading bars runs toward the viewer. It slides from west to east as the moon crosses the sky, on a row that swings nearer the viewer around midnight, and fades out over the jetty rather than jump across it. On open water each row wobbles by a pixel and the path by up to two; on ice it is half as bright and still. The sky itself is untouched.
- **The lamps in the water.** Two streetlamps stand close enough to reflect: the north lamp by the pitch and the west lamp. While a lamp is lit, its light hangs in the water below it as a thin column in the lamp's own amber: 1-pixel bars of uneven width, one every 2 pixels from 34 pixels below the lamp's foot, fading downward and each wobbling on its own. Bars that would fall over the shallows are left out, so each column has five to nine. They come on with the Lantern hour cascade, like every streetlamp. On ice the columns are half as bright and still.
- **The lanterns in the water.** The town's lanterns glimmer across still water. Small glints are scattered over the far water, 0.3 to 0.6 tiles out from the north shore, one for each lit lantern, from the same Lantern hour count. So they light one by one from 20:00 with the lanterns themselves and stay lit until 06:00. Their places light in a fixed, seeded order, so new glints appear here and there rather than sweeping along the bank. Each is a 2 × 1 fleck that shimmers and shifts by a pixel. There are at most 24; a larger town shows 24. They sit at least 4 pixels apart, leave the north lamp's column clear, and never show while the pond has any ice. See [The Lantern Fork](LANTERN_FORK.md).

## Keeping the football in view

The pond lies just below the Meadow Ground, so it is built low and to one side, and three rules keep every game in view. In world pixels, a tile at (x, y) projects to `sx = (x − y) · 38` and `sy = (x + y) · 19`.

1. **The north line.** Nothing the pond draws rises above the road between the pitch and the pond: every pixel keeps `sy ≥ 1102 + sx / 2`. The fans, the south floodlight masts, the refreshment cart, the rail and the scoreboard all stand above that line. Uprights near the north road also stay low enough to keep walkers' feet clear. The middle of the north bank has no reeds, which leaves the fans' foreground, the north lamp's column and falling goal confetti clear.
2. **The west line.** A courtesy to the west road and to the houses that may one day stand on G2, H2 and I2: every pixel keeps `sy ≥ 342 − sx / 2`.
3. **The framing rule.** The football panel frames the pitch with its own camera, which at 1440 × 900 sees world x from −758 to −2. No Millpond upright taller than 40 pixels may put any pixel at `sx > −770`. Only the mill is taller: its ridge reaches 88 pixels, and its silhouette stays between `sx` −900 and −770. That is why it stands on the south-west bank. The wheel does reach into the frame, so it stays under 40 pixels: its vertical radius is 20 pixels, and its rim tops out at about 39. The gate sign (28 pixels), the reeds (at most 20 pixels, 14 on the north bank), the heron (at most 32), the skaters and the boats all stay under 40 too.

The framing rule is a guarantee at 1440 × 900. A wider screen shows more of the west bank: at 1920 × 1080 the mill sits in the football panel's lower-left corner, never over the pitch, and every pond pixel in that frame still keeps to the north and west lines.

The tests enforce all of this. `tests/millpond-render.test.ts` records where every primitive of all three layers and the reed fireflies lands in world pixels, through every transform, and checks each point against the rules. It does so at ten stages of the year and nine minutes each, plus the secret, a heron strike and a fish leap. It repeats the check in the 1920 × 1080 football frame, where the mill must stay in the left 15% and the lower 45% of the screen. `tests/millpond.test.ts` checks the same rules analytically for every skate loop and approach, every boat loop and the heron's whole range, using sprite extents recorded from the art. In the fixture, `view=football&rules=1` draws the three lines over the app's football framing.

## Quiet on purpose

- **Amber stays with light.** The pond has no lanterns, lit windows or glow of its own. It never calls `drawGlow` and lays no wash over the screen. Its only amber is light that is lit at that instant: the two lamp columns and the lantern glints. Daylight glints on the water are pale and cool. The heron's bill is a muted ochre-grey, and the fish, the moon and the ice are silver, cream and blue-white. At night the gate sign takes the standard soft venue lettering, like the zoo's. See [the amber budget](BRAND.md#the-amber-budget).
- **One landmark.** The mill is the only tall thing on the site, and it stands to one side of the football.
- **No hints.** The panel and the skating card say what the water is doing now. They never mention what the coldest nights keep to themselves; that is in [Town secrets](TOWN_SECRETS.md).
- **No crowd promised.** The copy counts neighbours only while they skate, and never counts the scenery. Headings end with a period, eyebrows are uppercase, and there are no exclamation marks.

## Determinism

Everything on the pond is a pure function of the town day and minute, like the sky and the seasons: `(minutes + 1440, day − 1)` is the same moment everywhere. Whole-day gates (the ice, the boats' season, the lilies, the skating days) read the almanac's own date, so nothing can change a floating-point hair before midnight. Seeds come from `hash()` of fixed `millpond:` strings. The heron's plan for the day and each minute's fish rise are memoised, but both are pure, so no answer depends on what was asked before. There is no `Math.random`, no `Date`, no storage and no canvas created at module load, so node tests can import every module. The art's seeded tables (glints, snow, mist, petals, leaves and the lantern glints' places) are built on first use, not at import.

The skaters come from the published roster, so every visitor sees the same neighbours on the ice. Pausing freezes the pond with the rest of the view, and resuming catches up. Reduced-motion users inherit the town's paused start. The live broadcast uses the same renderer and the same skaters.

## Performance

Measured with the recording context in the opening view at 1440 × 900 (camera 720, 88, zoom 0.7), at ten stages of the year and nine minutes each:

- The pond draws 612–836 calls a frame across its three layers, against a budget of 2,200.
- Against the same date and minute in summer, the pond swings by −210 to +66 calls (budget −450 to +350). At night, counting the reed fireflies too, it never falls below −139 (budget −550).
- The largest parts are the mill, wheel and sign at 198 (106, 60 and 32; the sign's lettering is painted once), the reeds at 154 and the boats with their rowers at 145. The scenery skaters take 79, the fisher 43, the heron 41, the jetty posts 12 and a leaping fish 7. Every part has its own cap in the tests.
- The ground lives in the ground cache and reads only `night` and the season's whole day, so it repaints at most once per town day, like the farm. The terrain loop, the trees and the plot art skip the site.
- A whole opening-view frame stays between about 31,000 and 33,500 calls at the season tests' moments (31,497–33,461 with the Treeline), well inside the budget of `tests/render-budget.ts`, 41,400 calls with today's 18 houses. Against the same date and minute in summer, the whole frame differs by −754 to +287 calls.

## Code and verification

- `src/lib/millpond.ts`: the site and its geometry (the plots, the ground, the water outline, the gate, the shore path, the jetty, the mill, the reeds and the skate loops), the sightline rules, the ice (`iceOn`), and the model: `heronAt`, `fishRisesAt`, `boatsAt`, `fisherAt`, `sceneSkatersAt`, `mistAt`, `wheelAngleAt`, `iceSecretAt` and `millpondStatusAt`. It is the one source for the mill's outline (`MILL_SILHOUETTE`) and for the lily pads, petals and leaves (`lilyPadsOn`, `lilyFlowersOn`, `petalCountAt`, `leafCountAt`), so the art and the panel always agree. It also holds the skaters' seats, route and glide (`SEAT_LOOPS`, `loopForSeat`, `millpondRoute`, `skateGlide`). It imports nothing from the renderer, so the build-time schema can import it.
- `src/lib/millpond-copy.ts`: the pond's voice. It writes the panel's words for every status, the skating card, and the `PUBLIC SPACE · H3–I6 · 8 PLOTS` label.
- `src/city/millpond.ts`: the three layers. `drawMillpondGround` paints the cached ground: the bank, the path, the water, lily pads, ice, snow on the ice and the jetty deck. `drawMillpondSurface` paints flat art every frame: glints, the moon, the lamp columns, the lantern glints, petals, leaves, fish rings, wakes, the secret, mist and the selection outline. `drawMillpond` returns depth objects: reeds, jetty posts, boats, the fisher, the heron, leaping fish, the scenery skaters, the mill, the wheel and the gate sign, which is one piece sorted at its far end, so walkers on the road paint over it and keep their clicks.
- `src/city/season-palette.ts`: the `POND`, `ICE` and `REED` colours.
- `src/city/render.ts`: the terrain, tree and plot skips, the ground in the cache after the farm, the surface and objects after the cinema, and picking in `cityHit`: the water, the former roads and the mill under any roof in front, plus the gate sign. `src/city/weather.ts` adds the five reed swarms, and `src/city/residents.ts` the `skate` pose.
- `src/lib/resident-trips.ts` and `simulation.ts`: the skating visit and its labels. `live-director.ts` keeps a skater on the ice followable on `/live`. `town-config.ts`, `world.ts`, `events.ts` and `schema.ts` hold the reservation.
- `src/components/MillpondInfo.tsx` and `MillpondEventCard.tsx`: the pond's panel and the Events panel's skating card. `App.tsx` and `City.tsx` handle `#venue=millpond`, the camera and the tooltip.
- `tests/millpond.test.ts`: the reservation (exactly H3–I6, 141 house plots, the neighbouring plots still valid), roads, lamps, the water outline, the mill, the jetty and the path. The three rules checked analytically, the reeds, the ice matching the river tile for tile, and the wheel.
