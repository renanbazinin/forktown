# Expanding the town

The town has **300 plots: twenty rows (A–T) and fifteen columns (1–15)**, half as many again as the 200-plot town before it. B5 and C5 hold the stage and green; D3 holds the Lantern Fork; F3–F5 and G3–G5 form the six-plot Meadow Ground; D6, D7, E6, and E7 hold the Starlight Cinema; O4–R9 hold the 24-plot Willow Grove Zoo; S4–T9 hold the 12-plot Moon Harvest Farm; H3–I6 hold the eight-plot Millpond. The Riverside takes D14, D15, E14 and E15 for Market Square, J15 for the Boat Landing and K15 for the Bandstand. R1, N1, C1, A9, C15, L15 and R15 hold the seven halts of the Treeline. That reserves 70 plots and leaves **230 house plots**. Every existing house keeps its ID, coordinates, grass, and entrance; existing place JSON and share links need no migration.

The world is 64 tiles wide and 84 deep. The river runs down the east edge at x = 62 (x = 63 at its head, above y = 8), with the riverside road beside it at x = 61 and the far bank at x = 63. The Riverside's venues stand on the last column, between the riverside road and the water.

## One size setting

The town's size is one setting in `src/lib/town-config.ts`:

```ts
export const TOWN_SIZE = { rows: 20, columns: 15 } as const;
```

The next build derives the plot list, allowed contribution addresses, road graph, terrain edges, the river and map framing from those counts, and the builder and directory discover the new plots automatically. Restart the development server after changing it so browser and local-save validation use the same layout.

Growing the town is still planned work, not a one-line edit. Even one more row, `{ rows: 21, columns: 15 }` (315 plots, 245 of them for houses), turns tests red until it is done. Settle each of these in the same change:

1. **The Fork's capacity.** The Lantern Fork has 266 places: 8 for the founders and 258 for house plots (`FORK_SLOTS` in `src/city/lantern-fork.ts`, checked by `tests/lantern-fork.test.ts`). Its slots are fixed, not derived from `HOUSE_PLOTS`, so a town of more than 258 house plots needs another bough first; without one, the lanterns past the last slot are never drawn. `{ rows: 22, columns: 15 }`, with 260 house plots, is already past it.
2. **Wider towns.** More columns move the river, the riverside road and the far bank east with the edge. The Riverside (D14–E15, J15 and K15) and the bank halts (C15, L15 and R15) must move to the new last column in the same change, with their frozen spots, approaches and frames. Otherwise `src/lib/tubes.ts` throws "Tube station C15 is on no edge of town." when it loads, and the app and every test fail with it.
3. **Explicit pins.** `tests/district-places.test.ts` pins `TOWN_SIZE` and the 230 house plots, and `tests/contributions.test.ts` the 300 plots. Other counts that follow the plots, such as the timeouts, derive from `HOUSE_PLOTS`.
4. **Measured ceilings.** Any growth, even one row, moves measured ceilings, which must be measured and pinned again. At 21 × 15 these fail: `tests/journeys.test.ts` (the football ceiling of 209 minutes and the morning match's reach), `tests/outings.test.ts` (the turn-ticket counts a year), `tests/tubes-render.test.ts` (the whole town's 0.245 scale), `tests/bandstand.test.ts`, `tests/full-town-feel.test.ts` and `tests/trip-routes.test.ts` (lane and pose checks), `tests/event-seats.test.ts` (it crashes) and `tests/town-secrets.test.ts` (the farm's perimeter roads, once the farm is no longer on the last row).
5. **The canvas budget.** The whole full map already draws up to 142,653 canvas calls against the tests' cap of 150,000 (see below), so a bigger map probably needs terrain caching first.

## Compatibility rules

- Grow by adding rows and columns. Do not reduce either count in a published town: that could remove someone's plot or invalidate a saved draft.
- Scheduled public venues are configured in `src/lib/events.ts`, and the Riverside's in `src/lib/district-places.ts`; multi-plot sites are configured in `town-config.ts` (`FOOTBALL_SITE`, `CINEMA_SITE`, `ZOO_SITE`, `FARM_SITE`, `MILLPOND_SITE`, `MARKET_SITE`) and their site modules (`football.ts`, `cinema.ts`, `zoo.ts`, `farm.ts`, `millpond.ts`, and `tubes.ts`). Their plots must exist and remain reserved; the shared schema, builder, and directory exclude them from house contributions. Public sites remove their internal roads and lamps while preserving perimeter routes: Market Square loses the roads x = 57 and y = 17 inside it and keeps x = 53, x = 61, y = 21 and the duck street, y = 13. Single-plot venues and halts remove no roads or lamps. Never reserve an occupied house plot without explicitly relocating its contribution.
- **More rows** extend the west and bank runs of the Treeline and move nothing on the Riverside.
- **More columns** move the river, the riverside road and the far bank east with the edge. The Riverside's plots on column 15 (D14–E15, J15, K15) and the bank halts C15, L15 and R15 would then stand inland, and `tubes.ts` refuses a halt that is on no edge of town. So a wider town moves the Riverside and the bank halts to the new last column in the same change, with their frozen spots, approaches and frames, before anyone builds there.
- Keep the block spacing, coordinate origin, and address convention fixed. They define existing home positions. Changing them would require an explicit migration.
- Row letters and column numbers are independent of the current town size. Row Z is followed by AA, AB, and so on; A16 follows A15. Existing IDs are never renumbered.
- Place links use the place ID, not an array index or map coordinate. Leave existing place IDs intact.
- Walking destinations may change when roads grow, but resident data and simple routines remain compatible. Shoreline and border scenery move to the new edge.
- The duck family walks a fixed 40.7 tiles each way from the river along the Lunch Green street, so in a wider town they turn back before its far end instead of hurrying to reach it. At 64 tiles they turn at about x = 21.6, a little after 10:10.

## Before publishing an expansion

Run `npm run check`, `npm run format:check` and `npm run check:full-town`. The tests protect the original 25 plots, later growth along either axis, row labels beyond Z, road connectivity, new edge contributions, and shared schema validation. `check:full-town` runs the whole suite again with a made-up house on every free plot, so growth can't turn `npm run check` red for the next neighbor who moves in. Counts that follow the plots (house plots, timeouts) are derived from `HOUSE_PLOTS`; the explicit pins and the Fork's fixed slots are listed under [One size setting](#one-size-setting).

Inspect the full map and select the furthest new plot on desktop and mobile. Reset fits the whole map; the opening view frames the neighbors who live here, at a readable zoom; selecting a plot or following a neighbor zooms in so the town remains usable as it grows.

This configuration supports future expansion without scattering size constants across the app. It is not a promise of unlimited rendering capacity. A full 20 × 15 town draws about 133,000–137,000 canvas calls for the whole map at fit, and up to 142,653 in winter snow with all four snowmen (Winter 16 noon), against the tests' cap of 150,000 (`tests/district-render.test.ts`). It simulates its neighbors in under a millisecond a frame. Before growing further, profile the canvas and resident simulation with `check:full-town` and consider terrain caching or districts.

## Long-distance visits

Event trips use 0.32 tiles per town minute. The planner measures the road and venue path, aims to arrive five minutes early, and starts earlier when the resident has free time. Work, home, and sleep routines limit the available window. If a previous outing or work delays departure, residents may arrive late; trips that cannot reach the event before it ends and return before the next commitment are skipped, and the seat goes to the next neighbor in line who can make it. A trip must also be worth the walk: one way may take at most three times the minutes spent at the event (`WORTH_THE_WALK`). When necessary they leave the event early to get home on time. Return journeys use the same speed. A trip is planned for the whole day, so crossing noon, evening, or midnight never changes its destination or teleports its resident. Neighbors who would reach the same tube door or venue gate within two minutes of each other are spaced out, arriving up to six minutes earlier (else later) or leaving up to six minutes later (else earlier), so they never walk in as one figure. See [Town events](TOWN_EVENTS.md).

Far trips can ride the Treeline, one glass bore round three edges of town: down the west edge behind the trees, along the north edge, and down the far bank of the river. Its seven halts are Barley (R1), Willow (N1), Hedgerow (C1), Hawthorn (A9), Watercress (C15), Kingfisher (L15) and Bulrush (R15). A ride's fixed minutes are 2 to board (the short walk from the road to the halt's glass stack included), the glass at 10 tiles a minute, and 2 to step off and walk back out to the road: from 6.65 minutes (Barley to Willow) to 25.77 (Barley to Bulrush). A journey takes the tube only when, door to door, it is faster than walking the whole way by at least ten unhurried minutes, or 0.4 times the ride's fixed minutes where that is more. Only the walking legs hurry, never past 1.4 times the usual pace; the glass keeps its own speed. The way home is the same choice, never decided again. Nearby trips stay on foot. With every plot taken the worst door-to-seat trip to a daily venue, best of walk or tube, is 233 minutes (the zoo, from F12). See [The Treeline](TUBES.md) and `tests/manual/tubes.html`.

The zoo adds a second afternoon destination for some neighbors outside the green-s audience. Six habitats contain giraffes, elephants, zebras, penguins, and two planted but empty future enclosures. All 24 site plots are reserved, and visitors turn in under the stone gate at the north-west corner and use the side path and central promenade, outside the animal fences. What stands in each habitat (trees, the feeder, the elephant house, the zebra shade, rocks, logs, water, the penguin ledge) is listed once in `src/lib/zoo-layout.ts`: the art draws it and the animals keep clear of it. See `tests/manual/zoo.html` for the interactive zoo and far-away visitor preview.

In deep winter the frozen Millpond is one more. On Winter 9–19, half of the afternoon strollers whom the regatta, the fair, the lunch, the zoo and the football have not claimed, up to six, walk to its south gate and follow the shore path to their own loop. The loops in full view fill first, and nobody walks behind the mill. They skate from the moment they reach their loop, a few minutes before the posted 14:00, until 16:40 at the latest, then walk home. See [The Millpond](MILLPOND.md) and `tests/manual/millpond.html`.

The Riverside adds the rest. Market Square opens every morning for morning strollers, each with their own browsing hour, and the Bandstand plays a teatime set for afternoon strollers and a sundown set for evening ones. Regatta Week (Summer 10–16) sends paper boats down the river from the Boat Landing, the Harvest Fair and the Long Table (Autumn 23–25) open the farm's west end through its two fence gates, and on new-moon nights the night owls sit out on rugs on the Bandstand lawn to watch the stars. Every one of them seats on turn tickets, so each home's turn comes round whoever else moves in. See [Town events](TOWN_EVENTS.md#the-riverside) and `tests/manual/district.html`.

### Occasional animal moments

Zoo habitats take turns at their own pace: penguins every 113 real seconds, elephants every 137, zebras every 157, and giraffes every 173. Each turn lasts 30 seconds including approach and return, with the main trick lasting about 6-14 seconds. Animals rotate fairly within each habitat. Elephants drink and spray, penguins dive and swim lying flat, zebras run two laps and skid while the other two watch, and giraffes stretch for a leafy snack. Between turns each animal keeps to its own patch of the habitat: it walks somewhere, then grazes, looks around or just stands, and tails, ears, flippers and blinks never stop. Animals in one habitat always keep a clear gap, even during a trick or asleep; `tests/zoo-motion.test.ts` checks it over two town days. The shared town clock drives all movement and effects, including across midnight, so pausing and reloading cannot start a different performance. Use the four animal buttons in the zoo preview to jump to a close view of each sequence.
